# Contributing

The most useful contribution is a decision that exposes a failure in the method.

Before opening an issue, remove customer names, contact details, private financial records, credentials, and commercially sensitive information. Prefer a synthetic or explicitly permitted, redacted case. Do not publish a real conversation without permission.

For a proposed change:

1. Describe the decision, constraints, available evidence, and why the existing instruction fails.
2. Preserve the original no-skill and skill-assisted answers. Do not edit answers to strengthen a comparison.
3. Use the same model, tools, input evidence, and response budget in separate conversations. Record the versions and any deviations you actually know; do not invent missing metadata.
4. Separate development cases from unseen cases. Freeze the candidate before testing unseen cases. Choose a small test budget in advance rather than adding cases until it wins.
5. Where possible, ask a reviewer who does not know the labels to compare the answers. Record actionable differences, factual errors, ties, and tradeoffs. Extra detail or a different reasonable recommendation is not automatically a win.
6. Report limitations and the cost of longer instructions. Synthetic judgments do not establish actual revenue, customer demand, or general superiority.

Keep changes small and mechanism-specific. Avoid celebrity roleplay, fake quotes, universal scoring formulas, unsupported success rates, fixed interview-count gates, and mandatory multi-agent debates. Preserve the user's objective and a well-supported existing business when appropriate.

Write new material in your own words and cite primary methodological sources. Do not copy prompts or skill text from a repository without checking its license and applicable notices. Unlicensed source material may be discussed and linked; it must not be silently vendored.

Public issues and pull requests are visible to others. The project does not need your customer database or an API key to review a reasoning failure.
